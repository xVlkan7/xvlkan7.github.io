Ever since Elon's robotaxis started hitting parts of the country other than the bay area,
people have had a lot of opinions. Understandable! They represent a big change in the way we
interact with society. However, I think a lot of people have misguided opinions about these
pieces of tech, especially after I saw people vandalizing a Waymo a couple weeks ago.

## Initial Hesitation

Transportation as a sector is the largest employer in the US, and has a lot of different
parts associated with it. Whether its taxi drivers, truck drivers, private drivers,
public transportation, or any employees associated with those fields, self driving vehicles
pose an inherent economic/job risk to those roles. I don't doubt that this will be an issue in
the future, but it also shouldn't be the primary reason we limit technological growth.

Additionally, public transit investment would be much better than self driving vehicles for
a lot of reasons. However, given the state of public transit funding for the past 60 years
of US history, that isn't really something to rely on.

## Some Utopian Ideals

This is kinda the point of this whole post. I've been talking to people a lot about the possible
benefits to society of self driving vehicles, and most of them don't realize the benefits outside
of just "I can not turn my steering wheel on the highway/its a really good improvement on
lane keep assist" and there is so much more possible. Now, a lot of these are based on level ~5
self driving, essentially vehicles are so optimized that they no longer have a steering wheel,
forward facing cockpit, etc. Now there are a lot of safety issues not worked out yet, but I'm
going to be writing about utopian scenarios where these eventually get fixed.

### In a world of car ownership...

Lets talk about a world where people still own cars but are self driving. Lets start with a family
of 5, who all share one vehicle. Normally, this would be pretty much impossible. Sharing/coordinating
rides would take too much time and people would be left waiting forever for a ride. However, with
a self-driving car, this becomes very livable! In the morning, the car operates as a "bus", dropping
off every at their destination in order that they need to be dropped off. Without the need
for forward-facing seats, the family could eat breakfast together while in the car, reducing on
percieved commute time. First, the car stops at the school and drops off all the kids, then heads to
the office to drop off one of the parents. Then, it finally goes to the last destination, the doctor's
office for the other parent. While they are at the doctor's office, they place an order for curbside
pickup at the local grocery store. Almost all stores across the country already have this feature for
customers. While the appointment is happening, the car drives to the grocery store, opens the trunk,
and picks up the groceries. Then, it drives back to the doctor's office and picks up the parent.
At this point, the car can drive home, and on the way, pick up the kids from school. When everyone
arrives, the groceries are unloaded, and the car can either sit idle or charge before going back to
the office to pick up the other parent at the end of the day. Now, once the family is home, the car
could then go out and possibly earn some money as an autonomous taxi. This is just one example of
how useful in day to day life a self driving car could be!

### Without car ownership...

What about a scenario without cars? Many public transit/urbanist enthusiasts (myself included) have
envied the nordic countries' general lack of cars and communal spaces. In a world where car ownership
isn't as necessary as it is today in the US, how could we design cities/urban plan without all the
parking? Assuming that we could have self-driving cars act as a cheap taxi service for everyone as
the marginial cost of driving one around would be much lower than it currently is today. If you didn't
have to afford the initial purchase cost, insurance, maintenance, gas, possible parking costs, etc.
of a car, what else could you do with that money? Imagine how much space we could save/land could be
reclaimed by removing all the parking? This not only applies to living places, but also offices,
grocery stores, medical offices, sports stadiums, bars, and where people gather in general.
Public transit could also get more funding, as ridership would increase across the board.
Downtowns would begin to thrive again where they've been slowing down,
and more communities could be build around walkable areas. If this self driving data was anonomyized
and made accessible, transit providers could get insights into where to build bus routes, especially
among commuters that use common routes.

### What about those bus routes?

Public transit access to self driving vehicles presents a really cool opportunity. Now, a lot of people
jump to "automate all the buses!", which is good instinct, but comes with problems. Bus drivers do
a lot more than drive, like help passengers/people with disabilities, keep peace on the bus, and help
cyclists load bikes onto the bus if necessary. However, they are a high cost and can be one of the
limiting factors for new bus routes. This, however, could be offset by smaller self-driving Zoox-like
vehicles. For example, in Pittsburgh, PRT runs a good number of bus lines that only really make sense
to run during rush hour, however they can't just run those buses from 7-10 and 3-6 every weekday
as some people still depend on those lines. Well, imagine running a full size bus during rush hour,
but then running a smaller, autonomous van that carries a lot less people but also costs less to run
for off-peak hours? Certain bus lines could easily become 24-hour service and even have some service
on holidays when normal bus drivers have days off! Additionally, for new bus lines, these smaller
vehicles could become a pilot for new routes without having to plan the complexity of a whole new
large bus to carry people.

### A quick notes on RVs and other leisure vehicles

Wouldn't it be cool to go to sleep in your mobile home, tell the computer to take you to a national park
800 miles away, and wake up at the park? I think this would greatly increase domestic tourism, and
even though it might make national parks a bit more overpopulated, it would at least support more
funding for our parks department and hopefully creation of new parks/areas to visit. It might also
increase the number of people doing the whole 'van life' stuff, but that is a whole different issue.

## To end it off...

I won't say that the world will end up perfect, and there are some very serious issues that need to
be resolved. For example, self-driving car data and security need to be controlled and regulated
pretty heavily. Self driving cars could also lead to an era of almost no public transportation, where
everyone has a car(s) or uses them like taxis. This could have seriously devastating effects on
our urban planning, communities, and just life in general. Additionally, cars may change from a
cool area where people can define themselves through car ownership into a kinda vague aerodynamic-blob
focusing on maximizing range. A lot of times I think back to the early part of Fahrenheit 451, where
the "jet-cars" didn't have a ton of description outside of the fact that you don't really interact
with the outside world. They drive too quickly to even see/understand what you're driving past,
and a world where that becomes the norm is not ideal.

I think theres a lot of opportunity out there. Not only for new companies, but also just experiencing
a different way to organize our society, especially not around the car - like we have been
since the 1960s.

Thanks for reading!
