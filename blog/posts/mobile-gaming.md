Just the other day, I heard about No Man's Sky, a game that I have known since launch, just got an iOS release. So many games are coming to phones now that they have the power of last gen consoles all in a mobile form factor and batteries that can actually do a lot more work than you might realize. People have also gotten games like GTA V to work on mobile, which is an incredible feat.

### What mobile gaming used to be

I remember when people talked about how markets have changed for gaming - that mobile was the future, and consoles were a thing of the past. I was skeptical, and I think I was right at that time. Most mobile games were things that you would play at the bus stop, waiting in line, or just to pass time. Think a digital version of a sudoku book, but with more features. Games like Candy Crush were bringing in the big money, and there wasn't anything special. Bright graphics, easy UI, simple game mechanics, and easy progression that allows anybody to pickup the game. Now, gaming on a device can mean something completely different.

### "Real" mobile gaming

Lots of games have tried to have mobile ports, to varying success. Call of Duty mobile, for example, was an attempt at that. PUBG mobile, however, grew to insane popularity. Now I think the mobile interface still has real struggles - specifically, with internet connection as most people playing on the go won't have a reliable enough signal for playing certain multiplayer games. Technology can improve this, but a stationary PC/console is still in a much better scenario than a moving phone, possibly in a vehicle. But now that single player games like GTA V are available on mobile, what does that mean for portable gaming? What about game streaming, which planned to hit portable gaming? What about things like the Steam Deck? Also, just like we have Xbox and Playstation exclusives, will we see mobile-only versions? Will developers try to optimize games for phones, just like how Minecraft has a different "Pocket Edition"? (Idk if its bedrock or what anymore - haven't played PE in like 10 years lol).

### AI

Well, I think we all saw this coming. But one of the things I really want to highlight is people getting their hands on decompiled games and modding games. I choose my words specifically there - _people_, not just developers who use some of their free time to write mods. Anyone who has access to a free OpenAI/Anthropic account can have it write them a mod for a game, whatever they dream of! This is a nightmare for compatibility/reliability, but also, you can use the AI to check crashes and optimize the mod _specifically_ for your PC, with almost no thought involved! Its been really cool to see projects like ReSkate take the skate game that, even though I really wanted to love it and played probably 30+ hours of, was just underwhelming. It really highlights modern gaming today. Soulless, focused on profit (even though the devs have good intentions - shareholders/board meetings call the shots), and something aimed at everyone to enjoy. Its ok to make a game for a specific audience! Lots of games now try to cater to everyone, and it makes them fall short of something that could be really well done for a specific audience.

### To conclude

I'm really optimistic about gaming. Sure, people are making AI clones of games, and AI art is a real issue. I think those things will take time to iron out, and some conclusions are starting to surface. Game clones have always been a thing - when Fall Guys came out at $19.99 (I think), Stumble Guys, a knockoff was right there to play for free! Sure, Stumble Guys took some players, but if you played more than two hours, the investment for Fall Guys was easily worth it. As long as we don't lose our creativity, gaming has a bright future ahead.
